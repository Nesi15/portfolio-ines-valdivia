01

From sketch to character

Melboss Music · chatbot mascot and stickers · October 2026 · image

Problem

Melboss Music, the music-technology company I work for, needed a mascot for its chatbot and to use as stickers in its content. The challenge: putting it in any pose without it turning into a different character.

My role

Concept, pose sketch, direction and selection. Workflows built with Claude Code.

During the creative process, the goal was to choose an animal connected with music, one that sings. I considered birds, but they allowed little variety of poses and, for a chatbot, I preferred an animal closer to a human figure. That is why I chose the gibbon: it sings, its long arms give plenty of room to gesture (waving, thinking, pointing), and it can be humanised while staying a friendly animal.

The workflow

  1. DesignSDXL base · KSampler · batch 4

    Fast exploration: four variants per run to pick the character.

  2. Pose with ControlNet scribbleLoadImage → ImageInvert → SetUnionControlNetType (scribble) → ControlNetApplyAdvanced

    A hand-drawn scribble, inverted, enters ControlNet Union in scribble mode and drives the pose; the prompt sets the look.

  3. New pose, same characterFlux Kontext · ReferenceLatent · FluxGuidance

    Edits the reference image with an instruction ("now singing into a microphone") instead of generating from scratch.

Before and after

Scribble input for ControlNet My workGenerated pose AI-generated

What failed and limits

  • ControlNet follows the pose but not the proportions: the body comes out longer and simpler than the chosen design.
  • Automatic background removal left dirty edges, so I removed it from every workflow.
  • The result looked too childish for a brand. That led to experiment 02.

What I would do next

Train a character LoRA on the best images to lock its identity across models.

02

One character, four gestures, looping

Melboss Music · chatbot · October 2026 · image and video

Problem

The 2D version looked childish, and Melboss needed a corporate mascot for its chatbot: a 3D version performing complete actions (waving, thinking, getting an idea) and moving between them without cuts.

My role

Art direction (from cartoon to corporate mascot, palette, headphones on the shoulders, etc.) and choice of example actions useful for a chatbot. Workflows and player built with Claude Code.

The workflow

  1. 3D designZ-Image Turbo · ModelSamplingAuraFlow · KSampler

    Four proposals; one becomes the reference.

  2. ExpressionsFlux Kontext × 3 branches · ReferenceLatent

    One reference, three instructions in parallel: only pose and expression change.

  3. LoopsWanFirstLastFrameToVideo · FrameInterpolate (FILM)

    Each 5 s clip starts and ends on the rest pose, so clips chain without cuts. FILM doubles 16 to 32 fps.

Before and after

Before: 2D, childish AI-generatedAfter: 3D corporate mascot AI-generated

Player like the chatbot's: the rest loop repeats and every action returns to rest on its own. AI-generated

What failed and limits

  • First attempt: an HTML page crossfading still images. I dropped it because the character jumped from pose to pose.
  • Wan adds things nobody asked for: one version of the wave grew a tail, which gibbons do not have, and had to be regenerated.
  • Kontext sometimes put the headphones on the ears or tinted the eye area blue; I fixed it in the prompt.
  • 512 × 512 px: the most 8 GB of VRAM allows with these models.

What I would do next

Higher resolution on a GPU with more memory, an identity LoRA to stop relying on the prompt, and wiring the loops to the chatbot's real state (listening, thinking, answering).

03

Extending an illustration without breaking its style

Ferrovial · Innovate Construction Awards (ICA) 2026 · event posters and animation · October 2026

Problem

For the awards animation, the vertical illustrated poster had to become a horizontal universe: more forest, drones, cranes and people, with the same watercolour on paper and no visible seams.

My role

The method (one workflow per element, launched from Photoshop connected to ComfyUI), art direction and the PSD composition. Workflows, custom nodes and debugging with Claude Code.

The workflow

  1. Selection in PhotoshopPhotoshop ComfyUI Plugin → InpaintCropImproved

    I draw a selection where I want the element; it is cropped with surrounding context.

  2. Style and continuityJuggernaut XL · IPAdapterAdvanced · ControlNet Union «repaint» · DifferentialDiffusion

    IP-Adapter takes the watercolour from a reference cut from the poster; ControlNet continues what surrounds the selection.

  3. Back onto the paperPigment on paper (custom node) → InpaintStitchImproved → Send to Photoshop (custom node)

    Only the new paint is multiplied over the original paper, like a real wash, and returns to Photoshop as a layer.

Six workflows with the same structure (forest, bridge, people, drone, crane, Lego), each with its own style reference and an editable base description.

Before and after

First version: bug, the selection comes out black (NaN) AI-generatedFinal version: continuous forest on the paper AI-generated

Debugging the workflow

The first version returned the selection in black: decoding produced NaN values. Saving every intermediate step, I saw that the crop and mask were fine and that the failure appeared when ControlNet was on. This ControlNet Union (by xinsir) expects the area to fill to be marked with black pixels, and the preprocessor marked it differently; enabling that option (black_pixel_for_xinsir_cn) removed the black. A rectangle of a different tone remained, solved first by starting from the paper itself and finally with the "Pigment on paper" node.

What failed and limits

  • The plugin's "SendTo Photoshop" node failed on my ComfyUI version (asyncio); instead of patching the plugin, I wrote a custom node that does the same job.
  • Large selections can produce more than one element, and sometimes a faint trace of the selection edge remains.
  • Juggernaut with IP-Adapter imitates the poster illustrator's line. I used it with the illustrator's and the client's permission, and only to extend their own universe.

What I would do next

Measure style consistency between elements objectively, for example with distances between image embeddings, instead of by eye.

04

Animating an illustration without redrawing it

Ferrovial · Innovate Construction Awards (ICA) 2026 · event intro · October 2026

Problem

The event intro had to show the universe being drawn, from sketch to pencil to watercolour, during a camera pan, without the characters moving or changing.

My role

Concept and direction of the animation (continuous pan, the paper fixed while the camera moves, seeing each stroke drawn, pacing) and the change of method once video AI proved unsuitable. Python engine and Wan tests developed with Claude Code.

The workflow

  1. First attempt: video AIFlux Kontext → WanFirstLastFrameToVideo × 3 → FILM

    Four keyframes (sketch with dimensions, pencil, watercolour, final) and Wan animates the transitions.

  2. The PSD is the sourcepsd_a_escena.py → escena.json

    A script reads the PSD groups (forest, bridge, people, drone, crane, Lego) and writes the scene. Whatever I change in Photoshop shows up in the video.

  3. Animation enginehorizontal.py · Pillow · NumPy · ffmpeg

    Each element goes from pencil to watercolour on its own schedule, the camera follows keyframes and drones and cranes have their own loops. Half-resolution drafts render about 4 times faster.

Before and after

Pencil stage My work + AIFinal My work + AI

All the videos in experiments 03 and 04 are work-in-progress tests. The final result delivered to the client cannot be published.

What failed and limits

  • Wan redraws the illustration on every frame: characters move, strokes shimmer and dimensions become illegible. I dropped it for the final result.
  • Early renders took too long to iterate on. Describing the scene as data and rendering drafts solved it.
  • The extra forest on the sides was generated once with Adobe Firefly; everything else is poster layers and elements from experiment 03.

What I would do next

Move to true 2.5D with estimated depth, and a visual timeline editor instead of editing JSON.
